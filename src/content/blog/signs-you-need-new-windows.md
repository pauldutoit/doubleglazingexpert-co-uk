Not every window fault means a new window. A blown sealed unit, a worn seal or a broken hinge can be repaired for a fraction of a replacement. What cannot be repaired is a frame that has rotted, warped or cracked. This guide sorts the common signs into "fix it" and "replace it", and clears up the condensation question that trips people up.

## Points to take away

- Misting between the panes: reglaze the unit, keep the frame.
- Draughts and stiff opening: usually a worn seal or hinge, both cheap fixes.
- Rot, warping, cracked frames, or a frame pulling from the wall: replace.
- Condensation on the inside surface of the glass is a humidity problem, not a broken window.
- Several failing windows at once, or frames past 20 years: do the house in one order.

Reading time: about 6 minutes.

## Signs a repair will fix

These are common, they look worse than they are, and a glazier fixes them without a new window.

- **Misted or foggy glass.** The seal on the sealed unit has failed and moisture is trapped between the panes. The frame is fine. A new unit fitted into the existing frame is £70 to £150. Our guide to [misted double glazing](/blog/misted-double-glazing/) covers it in full.
- **A draught near the opening edge.** The rubber gasket has gone hard and flat, or the sash has dropped so it no longer presses on the seal. New gaskets or a hinge adjustment, under £100 in most cases.
- **Handle or lock not working.** Espagnolette rods, gearboxes, handles and hinges are stock parts. A window that will not lock is a security issue but a quick repair.
- **Water pooling on the inside sill.** Often the drainage slots in the outer frame are blocked with dirt. Clearing them stops the leak.

## Signs the window needs replacing

- **Rot in a timber frame.** Soft, spongy wood, especially at the bottom corners, means water has been getting in. Filler is a stopgap. Once the joints have gone, replace.
- **Warped or bowed uPVC.** Frames that have distorted in the sun no longer seal or lock properly and cannot be trued up.
- **Cracked frame or a cracked weld at a corner.** uPVC frames are welded at the corners. A split there lets water into the reinforcement and there is no repair.
- **The frame has moved away from the wall.** Gaps you can see daylight through, or that let water track down the inside, mean the fixing has failed and the window is coming out.
- **Old metal frames with no thermal break.** Single-glazed steel or aluminium frames from before the 1990s draught, corrode and run with condensation. Replacement is the only real fix.
- **Past 20 years and several are failing together.** At that point, repairing one at a time is throwing money at a job that is coming anyway.

## Condensation: which kind you have matters

This is the sign people misread most often.

**Between the two panes of glass.** You cannot wipe it off because it is sealed inside. The unit seal has failed. This is a window fault and needs reglazing.

**On the room-facing surface of the glass, on cold mornings.** You can wipe it off. This is warm, humid indoor air hitting a cold pane. It points to moisture in the house and not enough ventilation: drying washing indoors, a bathroom without an extractor, trickle vents closed. New windows can make it slightly worse at first, because a well-sealed house traps more moisture. The fix is ventilation, not new glass.

**On the outside of the glass in the morning.** This is a sign your windows are working well. The outer pane is staying cold because heat is not leaking through it.

## Score it: repair, replace one, or do the house

- **One misted unit, one stiff window, sound frames:** repair. A few hundred pounds.
- **Two or three faults, frames under 15 years and solid:** repair each. Cheaper than replacing.
- **Frames past 20 years, more than a couple deteriorating, or any rot or warping:** replace, and price the whole house at once for the volume discount and a single certificate. See the [cost guide](/blog/double-glazing-cost-uk/).
- **Selling within a year:** new windows if the current ones are visibly tired, because buyers notice.
