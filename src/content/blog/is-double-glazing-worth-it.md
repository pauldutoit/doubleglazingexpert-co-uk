Double glazing is worth it if you have single glazing or failing units, or if the windows need replacing anyway. If you already have working double glazing, the energy saving from replacing it rarely pays the cost back within the windows' life. The honest reasons to do it are usually comfort, condensation, noise and security, not the number on your energy bill.

Here are the figures, and the case for and against.

## Points to take away

- Single to A-rated double glazing saves roughly £140 to £285 a year, depending on house size.
- On energy alone, that payback runs 20 to 40 years, close to the life of the windows.
- Replacing working double glazing saves far less, often under £90 a year.
- The stronger reasons are draughts, cold spots, condensation, noise and security.
- Triple glazing rarely earns its premium in most UK homes.

Reading time: about 6 minutes.

## What double glazing saves on energy

These are typical figures for replacing **single glazing** with **A-rated double glazing** in a gas-heated home, based on Energy Saving Trust estimates.

| Property | Typical annual saving | Typical CO2 saving |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mid-terrace | £110 to £140 | 300 to 400 kg |
| Semi-detached | £140 to £195 | 400 to 500 kg |
| Detached | £195 to £285 | 500 to 700 kg |
| Bungalow | £120 to £170 | 350 to 450 kg |

Savings are lower with electric heating priced per unit, and lower again if the house is already well insulated elsewhere.

## The payback, honestly

Take a semi with 9 windows. A uPVC job is £4,000 to £6,000. An annual saving of £170 means a payback of **24 to 35 years** on energy alone. uPVC frames last 20 to 30 years. So on pure energy maths, it roughly breaks even over the life of the windows, and only if you started from single glazing.

Now take a house that already has **1990s double glazing** that still works. New A-rated units might save £60 a year. Against a £5,000 spend, that is a payback measured in decades beyond the life of the product. The energy case is not there.

This is why the "it pays for itself" line is misleading for anyone replacing working double glazing. It does not.

## When it is clearly worth it

- You have **single glazing**, especially with metal or timber frames that draught.
- Your double glazing has **misted or blown units**, or frames that no longer seal.
- The windows are **at the end of their life** and will need doing in the next few years regardless.
- You are **selling** and old or foggy windows are putting buyers off.
- **Noise, security or condensation** is a daily problem you want fixed.

## When the maths does not stack up

- You have **sound double glazing** under 20 years old with no misting or draughts.
- You are choosing windows purely as an **energy investment** and expect them to pay back fast.
- The rest of the house is **poorly insulated**, in which case loft and wall insulation give a better return per pound spent first.

## The reasons that are not on the energy bill

Most people who are glad they did it name these, not the savings.

- **Comfort.** No more cold radiating off a window or a draught you feel from the sofa. The room is usable right up to the glass.
- **Condensation.** A warm inner pane does not run with water on cold mornings, which cuts the damp and mould that follows.
- **Noise.** A sealed double unit knocks a noticeable amount off traffic and neighbour noise. Acoustic laminated glass does more.
- **Security.** Modern multi-point locks and internally beaded units are far harder to force than an old single-glazed sash.
- **Maintenance.** uPVC and aluminium need only cleaning, replacing years of painting timber or freeing seized metal frames.
- **The sale.** Buyers treat working double glazing as a given. New windows remove an objection rather than adding a premium.

## Double or triple glazing?

Triple glazing adds a third pane and costs £150 to £300 per window more than A-rated double. In most of the UK the extra saving is small, because A-rated double already cuts window heat loss a long way. Triple makes sense for new builds designed around it, very exposed coastal or upland sites, passive-house standards, or where noise is severe. For a typical home replacing old windows, **A-rated double glazing is the value pick**.
